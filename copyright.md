# Copyright and rights-complaint policy — pre-release draft

Status: published for internal testing and review preparation. This policy is not yet effective. Specialist counsel must approve the notice requirements, retention period, counter-notice or appeal process, and repeat-offender treatment before release.

Panel Pilot is operated by Aydin Daniel Sareff-Hibbert in New South Wales, Australia.

Panel Pilot does not host or index comic content and does not ship a source catalogue. It does allow users to install third-party compatibility extensions and make direct requests from their devices. Rights holders and authorised representatives must nevertheless have a clear private way to report an extension, repository, domain, or app-controlled listing that allegedly facilitates unauthorised access.

## Information a notice should identify

The final private reporting channel will request only information reasonably needed to assess the report, including:

- the reporter's name, organisation, contact details, and authority to act;
- identification of the protected work;
- the repository URL, package name, signer fingerprint, domain, or other precise target;
- an explanation of the complained-of activity and claimed rights basis; and
- any good-faith statement, accuracy statement, and signature required by applicable law.

Reports should be sent privately to [panelpilotcontact@proton.me](mailto:panelpilotcontact@proton.me) and should not include comic pages, credentials, reading history, or unrelated personal information. Do not post a formal notice as a public GitHub issue.

## Proposed response process

The operator will preserve the notice and decision record, promptly assess credible notices, avoid deciding disputed ownership questions without appropriate advice, and apply the narrowest effective signed-policy action. Available actions include marking a target under review; disabling aggregated discovery or new downloads; blocking networking for a package, signer, repository, or domain; and permanently revoking a target. Local user files and pre-existing downloads should not be searched or deleted merely because a network target is restricted.

Where appropriate and lawful, the extension operator should receive the substance of the complaint and a fair opportunity to respond. The effective policy must describe the final counter-notice or appeal process, restoration criteria, repeat-offender treatment, retention, and disclosure rules.

The current pre-release workflow is described in the [notice review and appeal process](appeals.html). It is an operational process, not a representation that Panel Pilot qualifies for any statutory safe harbour.

Panel Pilot does not claim any statutory safe harbour through this draft. Any such claim would require counsel to confirm eligibility and every legal and operational requirement, including any required registered agent.

Package signing, a safety review, or a rights-policy label is never proof that every item exposed by a dynamic third-party service is authorised.
