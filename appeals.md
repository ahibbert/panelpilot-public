# Notice review and appeal process — pre-release

Panel Pilot is operated by Aydin Daniel Sareff-Hibbert in New South Wales, Australia. This process is published for operational testing and specialist review. It does not claim a statutory safe harbour or replace rights available under applicable law.

## Submit a private notice

Send notices to [panelpilotcontact@proton.me](mailto:panelpilotcontact@proton.me). Identify the reporter and authority to act, the protected work or affected service, the precise package/repository/signer/domain or reviewed listing, the complained-of conduct, and a reply address. Do not attach comic pages, credentials, reading history, or unrelated personal information.

## Triage and interim action

The operator will record the notice, check that the target is identifiable, assess apparent urgency and credibility, and seek further information where reasonably necessary. A narrow interim action may mark a target under review, remove it from reviewed-software surfaces, disable aggregated discovery or downloads, or block a specific package, signer, repository, or domain through the signed policy. Panel Pilot will not remotely inspect or delete a user's local library.

These are operational targets rather than guaranteed deadlines: acknowledge a usable notice within two business days and complete an initial assessment within five business days. Credible urgent safety or rights concerns may receive an earlier interim restriction.

## Response and decision

Where lawful and appropriate, the affected software operator will receive enough of the allegation to respond without unnecessary disclosure of the reporter's personal information. Decisions should record the evidence considered, the scope and reason for action, the policy version, and a review date. Disputed ownership or legally complex matters may be referred for specialist advice.

## Appeal or request restoration

An affected operator or reporter may reply to the same monitored address with the original case reference, the challenged decision, corrected identity information, and supporting licence, authorisation, provenance, or technical evidence. A person not involved in the initial decision should review the appeal where practicable. Restoration requires a documented basis and a new signed-policy release; it is never automatic merely because an APK remains technically valid.

## Repeat concerns and records

Repeated substantiated complaints, evasion through replacement identities, false provenance, or refusal to address material risk may justify broader or longer restrictions. Records should be access-limited and retained only as long as reasonably needed for security, dispute handling, legal obligations, and auditability. Final retention and disclosure rules remain subject to specialist approval before public release.
